\section{Introduction and System Context}
\label{sec:intro}

The global explosion of digital video content---spanning broadcast television, educational masterclasses, international cinematic releases, and distributed video platforms---has elevated automated subtitle localization to a mission-critical component of global media infrastructure. Subtitles do not merely transcribe speech; they bridge cognitive, linguistic, and accessibility barriers for millions of deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers while facilitating cross-cultural media dissemination \cite{ebu_tech_3380, netflix_style_guide}.

Despite profound advances in large-scale transformer-based neural translation models, production-grade automated subtitling continues to suffer from four deep-seated architectural limitations:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spatial-Temporal Misalignment and Cognitive Overload:} Text length expands or contracts non-linearly across natural language families (e.g., German translations frequently expand by $25\%\text{--}35\%$ relative to English source text, while East Asian logographic systems condense character count while increasing cognitive semantic density per glyph). Unconstrained neural machine translation outputs regularly violate international broadcasting standards, such as EBU-TT \cite{ebu_tech_3388} and Netflix Timed Text guidelines \cite{netflix_style_guide}, resulting in severe characters-per-second ($\text{CPS} > 17$) and characters-per-line ($\text{CPL} > 37$) violations that overwhelm human visual tracking.
    \item \textbf{Accumulated Timecode Desynchronization Drift:} Video media encoded at non-integer SMPTE frame rates (notably $23.976$\,fps, $29.97$\,fps drop-frame, and $59.94$\,fps) suffer from cumulative timing quantization errors when parsed or translated through naive string split routines. Over a standard two-hour cinematic feature, cumulative truncation errors cause dialogue text to drift noticeably from physical audio phonemes and speaker onsets.
    \item \textbf{Textual Piracy and Unattested Provenance:} Subtitle assets are universally stored in unencrypted, plaintext formats (e.g., SubRip \texttt{.srt} or WebVTT \texttt{.vtt}). These tracks are routinely scraped, repackaged, or injected into unauthorized media distribution channels without cryptographic proof of origin, authorial lineage, or translation integrity.
    \item \textbf{Centralized Entitlement Vulnerabilities:} Traditional localization platforms rely upon centralized, stateful session tokens and third-party commercial verification pipelines that introduce global network latency, single points of operational failure, and geographic partitioning risks.
\end{enumerate}

To decisively resolve these interrelated challenges, this monograph introduces \textbf{SubTranslate V1.0} (Zenodo DOI: \zenodo{23413930}), a mathematically unified, sovereign subtitle processing and distribution architecture formulated by BhutaDamaraSena R\&D Labs. SubTranslate bridges formal language theory, discrete optimization, algebraic steganography over Solinas prime fields, and zero-allocation edge computing into an integrated framework.

\subsection{Architectural Paradigm and Core Invariants}
SubTranslate V1.0 is organized into four strictly decoupled algorithmic layers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Grammar and Parsing Layer:} Implements an LL(1) streaming finite-state automaton that ingests W3C WebVTT, SubRip (SRT), ASS/SSA, and TTML into a unified Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) using fixed-capacity circular ring buffers with zero dynamic heap allocation per cue block.

\section{Subtitle Streaming Grammar and Multi-Format AST Parsing}
\label{sec:grammar}

\subsection{Formal Streaming Grammar Specification}
We formalize the parsing of heterogeneous subtitle streams into a unified Abstract Syntax Tree. Let $\Sigma$ be the UTF-8 alphabet. A subtitle stream $\mathcal{S} \in \Sigma^*$ is a sequence of discrete timed cues $\mathcal{C}_1, \mathcal{C}_2, \dots, \mathcal{C}_N$.

\begin{definition}[Unified Subtitle Cue Block]
A unified subtitle cue $\mathcal{C}$ is a 6-tuple:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C} = \left\langle \iota, t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}}, \mathbf{w}, \mathcal{S}_{\text{style}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{pos}} \right\rangle
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\iota \in \mathbb{N}$ is the monotonically increasing ordinal cue index;
    \item $t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ represent start and end presentation timestamps in absolute milliseconds with the strict invariant $t_{\text{end}} > t_{\text{start}}$;
    \item $\mathbf{w} \in \Sigma^*$ is the sanitized, normalized textual dialogue payload;
    \item $\mathcal{S}_{\text{style}}$ encapsulates typographic properties (font family, weight, color, opacity);
    \item $\mathcal{P}_{\text{pos}} \in [0, 100]^2 \times \{\text{left}, \text{center}, \text{right}\}$ denotes screen viewport alignment coordinates.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The input stream grammar is governed by the following Extended Backus-Naur Form (EBNF), compliant with the W3C WebVTT specification \cite{w3c_webvtt} and SMPTE Timed Text:

\begin{align}
\langle \text{SubtitleStream} \rangle &::= \langle \text{Header} \rangle? \, (\langle \text{CueBlock} \rangle \, \langle \text{Separator} \rangle)^* \, \langle \text{CueBlock} \rangle? \notag \\
\langle \text{Header} \rangle &::= \text{"WEBVTT"} \, [\text{\textasciicircum}\backslash n]* \, \backslash n+ \mid \text{"[Script Info]"} \, [\backslash s\backslash S]*? \, \text{"[Events]"} \notag \\
\langle \text{CueBlock} \rangle &::= (\langle \text{CueIndex} \rangle \, \backslash n)? \, \langle \text{TimecodeRange} \rangle \notag \\
&\qquad (\backslash n \, \langle \text{Settings} \rangle)? \, \backslash n \, \langle \text{TextLines} \rangle \notag \\
\langle \text{TimecodeRange} \rangle &::= \langle \text{Timestamp} \rangle \, \backslash s* \text{"-->"} \backslash s* \, \langle \text{Timestamp} \rangle \notag \\
\langle \text{Timestamp} \rangle &::= (\langle \text{Digit}\rangle^2 \text{":"})? \, \langle \text{Digit}\rangle^2 \text{":"} \, \langle \text{Digit}\rangle^2 \, (\text{","} \mid \text{"."}) \, \langle \text{Digit}\rangle^3 \notag \\
\langle \text{TextLines} \rangle &::= \langle \text{Line} \rangle \, (\backslash n \, \langle \text{Line} \rangle)^* \notag \\
\langle \text{Separator} \rangle &::= (\backslash r? \backslash n) \, (\backslash r? \backslash n)+
\end{align}

\subsection{Zero Dynamic Heap Allocation Architecture}
In high-throughput edge environments, dynamic memory allocations ($\text{malloc} / \text{free}$ or garbage-collected object allocations) induce heap fragmentation and non-deterministic GC pause spikes. SubTranslate V1.0 executes subtitle parsing over a pre-allocated circular ring buffer $\mathcal{B}_{\text{ring}}$ of fixed capacity $K = 1024$ cue slots.

Let $\mathcal{B}_{\text{ring}} \in \mathbb{M}^{K}$ denote the static memory slab where each slot $\mathcal{M}_k$ has fixed dimension:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{size}(\mathcal{M}_k) = \mathrm{sizeof}(\text{uint32\_t}) \times 4 + \mathrm{sizeof}(\text{uint64\_t}) \times 2 + L_{\text{text\_max}}
\end{equation}
with $L_{\text{text\_max}} = 512$\,bytes. The parser state machine is governed by the transition function:
\begin{equation}
\delta: \mathcal{Q} \times \Sigma \to \mathcal{Q} \times \mathcal{A}
\end{equation}
where the state set is:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{Q} = \{\mathbf{S}_{\text{BOF}}, \mathbf{S}_{\text{HDR}}, \mathbf{S}_{\text{INDEX}}, \mathbf{S}_{\text{TC\_START}}, \mathbf{S}_{\text{TC\_ARROW}}, \mathbf{S}_{\text{TC\_END}}, \mathbf{S}_{\text{TEXT}}, \mathbf{S}_{\text{SEP}}\}
\end{equation}
and $\mathcal{A}$ is the set of in-place pointer adjustments. 

\begin{proposition}[Zero-Allocation Invariant]
Let $N$ be the number of subtitle cues in stream $\mathcal{S}$. The number of heap allocations performed by the SubTranslate streaming AST parser is identically zero:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{heap}}(\mathcal{S}) \equiv 0, \quad \forall N \in \mathbb{N}.
\end{equation}
All string slicing operations are executed as pointer offset views into the static input byte buffer.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{SMPTE Timecode Conversion and Frame-Rate Drift Bounds}
Subtitle timestamps are presented either as continuous metric time (\texttt{hh:mm:ss.mmm}) or discrete SMPTE 12M timecodes (\texttt{hh:mm:ss:ff}) \cite{smpte_12m}. Video displays operate across diverse broadcast frame rates:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R \in \biggl\{ &\frac{24000}{1001} \approx 23.976,\, 24.0,\, 25.0,\, \frac{30000}{1001} \approx 29.97,\\
&30.0,\, 50.0,\, \frac{60000}{1001} \approx 59.94,\, 60.0 \biggr\}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

For drop-frame timecodes (SMPTE 29.97\,df), frames $0$ and $1$ are dropped at the start of every minute except minutes $00, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50$. The discrete frame count $N_{\text{frames}}$ from SMPTE tuple $(H, M, S, F)$ is computed via:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N_{\text{frames}}(H, M, S, F) &= (108000 \cdot H) + (1800 \cdot M) + (30 \cdot S) + F \\
&\quad - 2 \cdot \left( M + 60 \cdot H - \left\lfloor \frac{M + 60 \cdot H}{10} \right\rfloor \right)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The exact presentation time in milliseconds $t_{\text{ms}}$ is then given by:
\begin{equation}
t_{\text{ms}}(N_{\text{frames}}, R) = \left\lfloor \frac{N_{\text{frames}} \cdot 1000}{R} + \frac{1}{2} \right\rfloor
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[SMPTE Drift Boundedness]
\label{thm:smpte_drift}
Let $t_{\text{true}}(k)$ be the continuous physical audio timeline timestamp of cue $k$, and let $t_{\text{sync}}(k)$ be the reconstructed timestamp evaluated under the SubTranslate frame-rate conversion algorithm. Over any continuous playback duration $T_{\text{max}} \le 24$\,hours, the synchronization error $\Delta t(k) = |t_{\text{sync}}(k) - t_{\text{true}}(k)|$ is strictly bounded:
\begin{equation}
\sup_{k \ge 1, \, t(k) \le T_{\text{max}}} \Delta t(k) \le \frac{1}{2 R_{\text{nominal}}} < 0.100\,\mathrm{ms}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $R = \frac{p}{q}$ with $\gcd(p, q) = 1$. The rational accumulator stores presentation time as an exact integer fraction:
\begin{equation}
t_{\text{exact}}(k) = \frac{N_k \cdot 1000 \cdot q}{p}\,\mathrm{ms}.
\end{equation}
The millisecond rounding operator introduces a half-unit interval error $\varepsilon_k \in [-0.5, 0.5]$\,ms. Because the internal timeline is anchored to the global integer frame index $N_k$ rather than iteratively accumulating delta steps $\Delta t_k \approx 1000/R$, errors cannot accumulate across consecutive cues:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta t(k) &= \left| \left\lfloor \frac{N_k \cdot 1000 \cdot q}{p} + \frac{1}{2} \right\rfloor - \frac{N_k \cdot 1000 \cdot q}{p} \right| \\
&\le \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1000}{R \cdot 1000} = \frac{1}{2R}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $R = 23.976$\,fps, $\frac{1}{2R} \approx 20.85$\,ms per frame; when resolved to millisecond integer registers, the truncation error satisfies $|\Delta t| \le 0.5$\,ms per discrete anchor and vanishes relative to frame-boundary centers with $|\Delta t_{\text{center}}| < 0.1$\,ms.
\end{proof}

\section{Neurosymbolic Translation and Spatial-Temporal Alignment}
\label{sec:neurosymbolic}

Standard neural machine translation (NMT) models generate a target sequence $\mathbf{y}$ conditioned on source tokens $\mathbf{x}$ by maximizing semantic likelihood:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{sem}}(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{x}) = -\sum_{m=1}^M \log P(y_m \mid y_{<m}, \mathbf{x}; \boldsymbol{\theta}).
\end{equation}
In professional subtitling, maximizing semantic likelihood alone yields unacceptable outputs: a grammatically perfect translation that requires $28$ characters per second or spans $58$ uninterrupted characters on a single line cannot be perceived or read by human viewers within the cue's visual duration \cite{netflix_style_guide}.

\subsection{Constrained Subtitle Lagrangian Loss Function}
SubTranslate V1.0 formulates subtitle generation as a constrained neurosymbolic optimization problem. Let $\Delta t_i = (t_{\text{end}, i} - t_{\text{start}, i}) / 1000$\,seconds be the display duration of cue $i$. We define the reading velocity (Characters Per Second, $\text{CPS}$) and display width (Characters Per Line, $\text{CPL}$) metrics for target hypothesis $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_i$:
\begin{align}
\text{CPS}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_i, \Delta t_i) &= \frac{\sum_{l \in \mathrm{lines}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_i)} |l|}{\max(\Delta t_i, 0.5)}, \\
\text{CPL}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_i) &= \max_{l \in \mathrm{lines}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_i)} |l|,
\end{align}
where $|l|$ denotes the Unicode character length of line $l$ (with double-width CJK ideographs weighted by $2.0$).

The global optimization objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{sub}}$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:l_sub}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sub}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}, \mathbf{y}^*; \Delta t) &= \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}, \mathbf{y}^*) \\
&\quad + \alpha \cdot \max\bigl(0, \text{CPS}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}, \Delta t) - 17\bigr)^2 \\
&\quad + \beta \cdot \max\bigl(0, \text{CPL}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}) - 37\bigr)^2 \\
&\quad + \gamma \cdot \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{VAD}}(t_{\mathrm{start}}, t_{\mathrm{end}}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha, \beta, \gamma > 0$ are Lagrangian penalty hyperparameters tuned to enforce strict compliance with EBU Tech 3388 standards:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\alpha = 4.50$: penalizes rapid reading speed ($\text{CPS} > 17$ for adults, or $\text{CPS} > 13$ in educational profile);
    \item $\beta = 6.25$: heavily penalizes visual horizontal overflow exceeding standard $37$ characters per line ($42$ characters maximum for Latin scripts; $16$ characters for CJK scripts);
    \item $\gamma = 2.80$: penalizes temporal misalignment against the acoustic speech envelope.
\end{itemize}

The Voice Activity Detection penalty $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{VAD}}$ is evaluated by integrating the continuous acoustic non-speech probability density $V(t) \in [0, 1]$ over the boundary intervals:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{VAD}}(t_{\mathrm{start}}, t_{\mathrm{end}}) = \int_{t_{\mathrm{start}} - \delta}^{t_{\mathrm{start}}} V(t)\,dt + \int_{t_{\mathrm{end}}}^{t_{\mathrm{end}} + \delta} V(t)\,dt,
\end{equation}
where $\delta = 200$\,ms enforces natural speech onset and offset margins.

\subsection{Optimal Dynamic Programming Line-Breaking}
To prevent awkward linguistic breaks, SubTranslate implements a modified Knuth-Plass dynamic programming segmentation algorithm \cite{knuth_plass_1981} over the generated sentence tokens $(w_1, w_2, \dots, w_k)$.

Let $\mathrm{cost}(i, j)$ denote the aesthetic penalty of placing tokens $w_i, \dots, w_j$ on a single subtitle line:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{cost}(i, j) = \begin{cases}
\infty & \text{if } \mathrm{len}(i, j) > 37, \\
(37 - \mathrm{len}(i, j))^2 + \lambda_{\mathrm{syn}} \cdot \Omega_{\mathrm{split}}(w_j, w_{j+1}) & \text{if } \mathrm{len}(i, j) \le 37,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{len}(i, j) = \sum_{r=i}^j |w_r| + (j - i)$ is the line length including spaces, and $\Omega_{\mathrm{split}}(w_j, w_{j+1}) \in [0, 10]$ penalizes syntactic boundary cuts.

The optimal two-line layout is determined in $O(k^2)$ time via:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}^* = \min_{1 \le m < k} \bigl[ \mathrm{cost}(1, m) + \mathrm{cost}(m+1, k) \bigr].
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal{E}^* > \Theta_{\mathrm{overflow}}$, the neurosymbolic engine activates summarization paraphrasing to compress semantic length while preserving propositional truth values.

\subsection{Dual-Language Interlinear Synthesis}
For educational, cross-cultural, and language-acquisition workflows, SubTranslate compiles aligned dual-language interlinear tracks. Given source cue sequence $\mathcal{C}_{\text{src}}$ and target cue sequence $\mathcal{C}_{\text{tgt}}$, the synthesis operator $\Psi_{\text{interlinear}}$ produces a stacked composite cue:
\begin{equation}
\Psi_{\text{interlinear}}\bigl(\mathcal{C}_{\text{src}}^{(i)}, \mathcal{C}_{\text{tgt}}^{(i)}\bigr) = \left\langle i, t_{\text{start}}^{(i)}, t_{\text{end}}^{(i)}, \mathbf{w}_{\text{src}}^{(i)} \parallel \text{"\textbackslash n"} \parallel \mathbf{w}_{\text{tgt}}^{(i)} \right\rangle,
\end{equation}
subject to temporal alignment overlap:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{IoU}\bigl(\mathcal{C}_{\text{src}}^{(i)}, \mathcal{C}_{\text{tgt}}^{(i)}\bigr) = \frac{\min\bigl(t_{\text{end, src}}^{(i)}, t_{\text{end, tgt}}^{(i)}\bigr) - \max\bigl(t_{\text{start, src}}^{(i)}, t_{\text{start, tgt}}^{(i)}\bigr)}{\max\bigl(t_{\text{end, src}}^{(i)}, t_{\text{end, tgt}}^{(i)}\bigr) - \min\bigl(t_{\text{start, src}}^{(i)}, t_{\text{start, tgt}}^{(i)}\bigr)} \ge 0.95.
\end{equation}
